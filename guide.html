What's in the kit

PracticePay teaches earning and spending with a phone: tap a paycheck to get money, tap a price tag to buy. Every piece has an NFC chip inside, and there's nothing to install.

PieceQtyWhat it does
Access card1Opens the PracticePay app. The app's web address is also printed on it.
Paycheck card1Adds $10 to the account.
Price tags5Each one takes money away: $1, $2, $3, $4 and $5.

The account starts at $0. Tap the paycheck first to get money to spend.

Privacy: PracticePay has no accounts or sign-ups and collects no personal information. The balance is saved only on the phone.

Getting started

Setup takes about two minutes: open the app once with the access card, then you're ready to tap.

iPhone

  1. Unlock the phone. The screen must be on.
  2. Hold the access card against the top edge of the back of the phone.
  3. A banner appears at the top of the screen. Tap it, and PracticePay opens in Safari.
  4. Check that Safari isn't in Private mode (see Troubleshooting).

No tap? Type the web address printed on the access card into Safari instead.

iPhone 7, 8 or X: these phones don't read tags automatically. Go to Settings → Control Center and add NFC Tag Reader. Before each tap, open Control Center and tap the NFC button.

Android: turn on NFC first

NFC is often switched off on Android. The fastest way to turn it on:

  1. Swipe down from the top of the screen to open Quick Settings.
  2. Look for an NFC tile and tap it so it's highlighted. You may need to swipe left or tap the pencil icon to find it.

If there's no tile, use Settings. The location depends on the brand:

PhoneWhere NFC is
Samsung GalaxySettings → Connections → NFC and contactless payments
Google PixelSettings → Connected devices → Connection preferences → NFC
Motorola, OnePlus and othersOpen Settings and search for NFC

Then:

  1. Unlock the phone.
  2. Hold the access card against the middle of the back of the phone. Android's reader is usually there.
  3. PracticePay opens in the browser. If the phone asks which app to use, choose Chrome and Always.

No tap? Type the web address printed on the access card into Chrome instead.

Use one browser. The balance lives in the browser it was earned in. Tags always open in the phone's default browser, so use that same browser if you open the app by typing the address.

How to play

Tap a card to the phone the same way you tapped the access card. On iPhone, tap the banner that appears.

You tapScreenWhat it says
Paycheck cardGreen, with a dollar bill"You got paid! +$10. Now you have …"
Price tag, enough moneyBlue, with a checkmark"Done. You spent $3. You have … left."
Price tag, not enough moneyOrange, with a raised hand"Uh oh you don't have enough money, please put the item back." It can also show the price and the balance.
A card PracticePay doesn't knowGrey, with a question mark"I don't know this tag. Please ask for help."

Each message stays on screen until the player taps the big OK button. Hear it reads the message aloud. Phones only allow speech after a tap, so it can't talk on its own.

The main screen shows the money card with the balance and a Recent list of the last eight paychecks and purchases.

Accidental double taps are safe. The same card tapped twice within 4 seconds only counts once, and refreshing the page never charges again.

Caregiver settings

The caregiver area is locked behind a press-and-hold so players don't change things by accident. To open it, press and hold the lock icon in the top corner for 2 seconds; a green ring fills as you hold. Tap Done to return to the game.

Math ModeOff by default. When on, a paycheck asks "You had $10 and just earned $10 more. How much money do you have now?" and a purchase asks "You had $10 and you just spent $5. How much money do you have left?" The new total stays hidden until the player answers. Hints work the same way as the not-enough-money question.

Extra paychecks and price tags are sold separately as add-on packs.

How long the balance is saved

The balance stays through closing the browser, locking the phone and restarting it. On iPhone, it can be erased if PracticePay goes unused for about a week.

BrowserHow long the balance lasts
Safari (iPhone)Until cleared. Safari may erase it after about 7 days of browsing without opening PracticePay. Any tap or visit restarts that clock.
Chrome (Android)Until cleared. Chrome may remove it only if the phone runs very low on storage.
Private or Incognito tabsErased when the tab closes. On iPhone, each Private tab starts at $0.

The balance is also erased by clearing browsing history or website data, and it doesn't move between phones or browsers. Each phone is its own account.

Before a long break, such as school holidays, write down the balance. If it's lost, restore it in Caregiver settings with Set.

Troubleshooting

The most common problem is the browser being in Private or Incognito mode. A paycheck seems to work, but every price tag says "not enough money" and the balance shows $0.

Turn off Private browsing

iPhone (Safari):

  1. Tap the tabs button (two overlapping squares, usually at the bottom right).
  2. At the bottom, tap Tabs (it may show a number, like "3 Tabs") instead of Private.
  3. Tap a tab or Done, then tap the card again.

To stop Private mode coming back, go to Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions, turn it on, then choose App Store, Media, Web & Games → Web Content → Limit Adult Websites. This removes the Private option from Safari.

Android (Chrome):

  1. Tap the tabs button (a square with a number) at the top right.
  2. Switch from the Incognito side to your regular tabs.
  3. Close the Incognito tabs, then tap the card again.
